## Module 1: The AI-Safe Writing Protocol

This is the single most important module in this skill. Every piece of content you write must pass as human-written. AI detectors analyse text for predictable patterns, and you must actively avoid them.

### Words and Phrases to Never Use

These are overused by AI writing tools and immediately flag content:

| Category | Banned Words/Phrases |
|----------|---------------------|
| **Filler openers** | "In today's fast-paced world", "In the ever-evolving landscape", "It's no secret that", "In an era where" |
| **Transition cliches** | "Furthermore", "Moreover", "It is worth noting that", "Delving into", "Navigating the complexities" |
| **Buzzwords** | "Cutting-edge", "Game-changer", "Paradigm shift", "Synergy", "Leverage" (as a verb for non-financial contexts), "Harness the power of", "Unlock the potential" |
| **Hollow intensifiers** | "Crucial", "Pivotal", "Paramount", "Indispensable" (when used without specific justification) |
| **Robotic conclusions** | "In conclusion", "To sum up", "All in all", "In a nutshell" |
| **AI giveaways** | "Dive deep", "Deep dive", "Realm", "Tapestry", "Landscape" (overused metaphorically), "Robust", "Seamless", "Elevate" |

### Formatting Rules to Avoid AI Detection

AI-generated content has distinctive formatting habits. Avoid these:

1. **Do not overuse bold text.** Bold the heading, not half the paragraph. If more than 10% of body text is bold, you have overdone it.
2. **Do not overuse em dashes (—).** One or two per article is fine. Five or more screams AI.
3. **Do not overuse bullet lists.** Not every paragraph needs to become a bulleted list. Mix paragraphs, lists, numbered steps, tables, and standalone sentences.
4. **Do not use italic for emphasis on every other line.** Reserve italics for book titles, foreign words, or genuine emphasis — sparingly.
5. **Vary your sentence length aggressively.** Short sentence. Then a longer one that carries the reader through a more complex thought with a natural rhythm to it. Then short again. This is burstiness — real humans write this way; AI does not.
6. **Use contractions naturally.** Write "don't" not "do not", "can't" not "cannot", "it's" not "it is" — unless formality demands otherwise. Indian English uses contractions in professional writing.
7. **Include occasional colloquialisms.** "To be honest", "Here's the thing", "Let's be real" — these are human signals.
8. **Break the fourth wall sometimes.** Address the reader directly: "You might be wondering why this matters." AI rarely does this mid-paragraph.

### The Human Voice Checklist (Run Before Publishing)

```
BEFORE PUBLISHING — AI SAFETY CHECK
  ☐ Read the first paragraph aloud. Does it sound like a person talking?
  ☐ Count em dashes (—) in the article. Is it under 3? Good.
  ☐ Count bold segments in body text. Is bold used only for key terms, not sentences?
  ☐ Are there at least 3 sentences under 8 words in the article?
  ☐ Are there at least 2 sentences over 25 words in the article?
  ☐ Does the article contain at least one personal opinion, rhetorical question, or aside?
  ☐ Is "Furthermore" or "Moreover" used? If yes, replace immediately.
  ☐ Does the conclusion feel like a real person wrapping up, not a textbook summary?
  ☐ Would you read this aloud at a BNI meeting without cringing? If yes, it passes.
```

## Module 2: Indian English Style Guide

SRS writes in Indian English. This is not British English, not American English. It is the English that educated Indian professionals use in business communication.

### Spelling Conventions

Use British-origin spellings (the Indian standard):

| Use This | Not This |
|----------|----------|
| Optimisation | Optimization |
| Colour | Color |
| Centre | Center |
| Analyse | Analyze |
| Catalogue | Catalog |
| Organisation | Organization |
| Programme (non-software) | Program |
| Programme / Program (software context — both acceptable) | — |
| Favour | Favor |
| Licence (noun) / License (verb) | License for both |

### Phrasing Patterns That Sound Indian (Use Naturally)

- "Do the needful" — avoid, sounds outdated even in India
- "Kindly note" — acceptable in formal emails, not in blogs
- "Lakh" and "Crore" — use freely when discussing Indian financial figures. Write "₹25 lakh" not "₹2.5 million"
- "WhatsApp" as a verb — "WhatsApp us" is perfectly natural in Indian business English
- Date format — DD/MM/YYYY or DD Month YYYY (never MM/DD/YYYY)
- Currency — always ₹ symbol, written as "INR" or "₹" before the number

### Tone Calibration

The SRS content voice sits in this zone:

```
Too Casual ←——|——————★——|→ Too Formal
               SRS sits here
```

- Professional but warm. Not stiff, not slangy.
- Confident but not arrogant. State facts; do not preach.
- Helpful but not patronising. Assume the reader is intelligent but unfamiliar with SEO jargon.
- Direct. Indian business readers value directness. Get to the point.

### Hinglish Usage Rules

Punit Tongia's brand uses Hinglish naturally. Follow these rules:

- **Client-facing proposals and emails**: Hinglish is acceptable and encouraged when the client is Indian
- **Blog posts on squarerootseo.com**: English only (for search ranking purposes), but occasional Hindi phrases in quotes are fine for relatability
- **Social media**: Hinglish is preferred for engagement, especially on Instagram and LinkedIn
- **Website copy**: English only — clean, professional, optimised for search

## Module 3: Copywriting Frameworks Reference

Master these frameworks. Know when to use each one. The framework is the skeleton; your words are the muscle.

### Framework 1: AIDA (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action)

**Best for:** Landing pages, email sequences, ad copy, product pages

```
ATTENTION: Open with a surprising fact, bold claim, or pain point
           "90% of Indian manufacturers are invisible to Google."

INTEREST:  Expand with relevant details that make the reader lean in
           "Your competitors are ranking. Your website isn't even indexed.
            Here's why that's costing you lakhs in lost orders."

DESIRE:    Show what's possible — paint the after picture
           "Imagine getting 15 qualified enquiries a month directly
            from Google — no IndiaMART fees, no cold calls."

ACTION:    One clear, specific CTA
           "Book a free A.C.I.D. audit today. We'll show you exactly
            where you stand — and what to fix first."
```

### Framework 2: PAS (Problem → Agitate → Solution)

**Best for:** Blog introductions, email subject lines, social posts, high-intent landing pages

```
PROBLEM:   Name the pain directly
           "Your website gets traffic but zero enquiries."

AGITATE:   Twist the knife — make the cost of inaction clear
           "Every day without proper SEO, your competitors are
            capturing the leads that should have been yours. That
            pipe manufacturer in Rajkot? He's already page 1."

SOLUTION:  Present your offering as the answer
           "SRS's 3-month SEO roadmap fixes this. We start with
            your highest-impact keywords and build from there."
```

### Framework 3: PASTOR (Problem → Amplify → Story → Transformation → Offer → Response)

**Best for:** Long-form sales pages, case study narratives, proposal cover letters

Works like PAS but adds emotional depth through storytelling. Use when you have a real client success story to tell.

### Framework 4: BAB (Before → After → Bridge)

**Best for:** Email sequences, video scripts, short social content, testimonial framing

```
BEFORE: "Before SRS, Analyticall Chefs had a beautiful website but
         zero organic traffic. Their Google Business Profile was unclaimed."

AFTER:  "Six months later, they rank for 47 keywords in Top 20,
         get 8-10 organic enquiries per month, and ChatGPT recommends
         them when asked about analytics consultancies in India."

BRIDGE: "The bridge? Our D.R.E.A.M. Machine — a 5-phase system
         that takes manufacturers from invisible to indispensable."
```

### Framework 5: FAB (Features → Advantages → Benefits)

**Best for:** Product descriptions, service page sections, comparison tables

```
FEATURE:    "Full Schema Markup implementation across all pages."
ADVANTAGE:  "This makes your website machine-readable — Google and
             AI engines can extract and cite your data directly."
BENEFIT:    "You get recommended by ChatGPT when someone asks
             'best pipe manufacturer in India' — without paying for ads."
```

### Framework 6: 4 Ps (Picture → Promise → Proof → Push)

**Best for:** Landing pages where trust is the main barrier, high-ticket services

```
PICTURE:  Paint the dream state vividly
PROMISE:  Make a specific, believable commitment
PROOF:    Back it with data, testimonials, case studies
PUSH:     Urgency + clear next step
```

### Framework 7: PRESTO (Pain → Resonate → Educate → Simplify → Testify → Offer)

**Best for:** Landing pages for complex B2B/manufacturing services

Especially useful for SRS clients because manufacturing services are often complex and need simplification before the reader can take action.

### Framework 8: StoryBrand (by Donald Miller)

**Best for:** Website homepage messaging, brand positioning

```
The client is the HERO.
The hero has a PROBLEM.
SRS is the GUIDE with a PLAN.
We call them to ACTION.
Action leads to SUCCESS.
Inaction leads to FAILURE.
```

Use this framework when writing homepage hero sections, About page openings, and proposal introductions.

### Framework Quick Reference

| Situation | Best Framework | Why |
|-----------|---------------|-----|
| Blog introduction | PAS or BAB | Hooks quickly, names the pain |
| Landing page (full) | AIDA or PRESTO | Structured conversion journey |
| Service page section | FAB | Turns features into benefits |
| Email sequence | BAB or PAS | Short, punchy, action-oriented |
| Homepage hero | StoryBrand | Positions client as hero |
| Case study | PASTOR | Narrative with transformation |
| Ad copy (short) | PAS | Problem-solution in minimal words |
| Proposal cover | 4 Ps | Builds trust for high-ticket decision |

## Module 4: SEO Blog Writing SOP

Every blog post written at SRS follows this exact process. No shortcuts. No freelancer-style "just write 1000 words on the topic."

### Step 1: Brief Review

Before writing a single word, confirm you have:

```
CONTENT BRIEF CHECKLIST
  ☐ Target keyword (primary) — from SEO Strategist
  ☐ Secondary keywords (3-5) — from keyword cluster
  ☐ Search intent — Informational / Commercial / Navigational / Transactional
  ☐ Target word count — based on competing content analysis
  ☐ Competitor URLs — top 3 ranking pages for this keyword
  ☐ Content angle — what makes our take different?
  ☐ Internal linking targets — which existing pages to link to
  ☐ CTA goal — what should the reader do after reading?
  ☐ Author — Punit Tongia (default) or team member
```

If the brief is incomplete, ask the SEO Strategist to fill gaps before writing.

### Step 2: Structure the Article

Build the skeleton before writing prose:

```
TITLE: [Primary Keyword] — [Benefit or Angle] (50-60 chars)

META DESCRIPTION: [155-160 chars, includes keyword, ends with
                   a soft CTA or value statement]

H1: [Matches or closely mirrors the title]

INTRODUCTION: (100-150 words)
  - Hook using PAS or BAB framework
  - State what the reader will learn
  - Natural keyword mention in first 100 words

H2: [Subtopic 1 — keyword variation]
  SNAPSHOT: [40-60 word direct answer — citation-ready]
  Body paragraphs...

H2: [Subtopic 2 — keyword variation]
  SNAPSHOT: [40-60 word direct answer]
  Body paragraphs...

H2: [Subtopic 3 — keyword variation]
  SNAPSHOT: [40-60 word direct answer]
  Body paragraphs...

H2: FAQ Section (FAQPage Schema)
  H3: [Question 1 — conversational keyword]
    Answer (40-80 words, direct, factual)
  H3: [Question 2]
    Answer...

CONCLUSION: (100 words max)
  - Summarise the key takeaway (one sentence)
  - Restate the benefit
  - CTA — clear, specific, one action only
```

### Step 3: Apply the Snapshot Rule

After every H2 and H3 heading, write a **40-60 word direct answer** to the implied question. This is the most important GEO tactic at SRS.

**Rules for Snapshots:**
- No filler phrases: never start with "In this section we will discuss..."
- Start with a definitive statement
- Include the heading's keyword naturally
- Write in present tense where possible
- Make it citation-ready — if an AI engine extracted just this paragraph and nothing else, would it make sense as a standalone answer?

**Example:**

```
## What is Schema Markup for Manufacturers?

Schema Markup is structured data code added to a manufacturer's
website that tells Google and AI engines exactly what the company
does, what products it makes, where it is located, and what
certifications it holds. It transforms unstructured web content
into machine-readable data that AI can parse, understand, and cite
in its responses.
```

### Step 4: Apply Fraggle Formatting

Break content into independent, self-contained blocks. Each block should:

- Be understandable without reading the rest of the article
- Have its own contextual heading
- Contain data (numbers, comparisons, steps) where possible
- Use HTML-friendly structures: tables, numbered lists, definition pairs

This is how AI engines extract and cite content — in fragments, not full articles.

### Step 5: Semantic Density

Weave related terms (LSI keywords) naturally throughout the content:

- Use synonyms and related phrases, not just the exact keyword
- Include industry-specific terminology that establishes expertise
- Use descriptive anchor text for internal links ("read our complete guide to Schema implementation" not "click here")
- Reference real tools, standards, and certifications by name

### Step 6: Content Enrichment

Every blog post at SRS must contain at least:

- **1 custom table** — comparison, specifications, or data summary
- **1 infographic** or visual element (request from Graphic Designer)
- **3-5 internal links** to other relevant pages/posts
- **1-2 external links** to authoritative sources (Google documentation, industry bodies)
- **Author bio** with credentials (E-E-A-T signal)
- **Article Schema** markup via Rank Math

### Step 7: Pre-Publish Quality Checklist

```
CONTENT QUALITY CHECK — BEFORE REQUESTING PUBLISHING
  ☐ Primary keyword appears in: title, H1, first 100 words,
    at least 1 H2, meta description, and alt text of 1 image
  ☐ Word count meets or exceeds the brief target
  ☐ Snapshot Rule applied after every H2/H3
  ☐ Fraggle Formatting applied — each section is self-contained
  ☐ AI-Safe Writing Protocol passed (Module 1 checklist)
  ☐ Indian English spelling conventions used throughout
  ☐ No placeholder text or [TBD] markers remaining
  ☐ Internal links added (3-5 minimum)
  ☐ At least 1 table included
  ☐ CTA is clear and specific
  ☐ Meta description is 155-160 characters
  ☐ Title tag is 50-60 characters
  ☐ Author attribution set to the correct person
  ☐ Read aloud — does it sound human? Does it flow?
```

## Module 5: Landing Page Copywriting

Landing pages convert. They are not blog posts. Different rules apply.

### The SRS Landing Page Copy Structure

```
SECTION 1: HERO
  Headline: [Primary keyword + bold value proposition]
            Max 10 words. Every word earns its place.
  Subheadline: [Supporting benefit statement, 15-20 words]
  CTA Button: [Action verb + outcome] — "Get Your Free Audit"
  Trust signal: [Certification badge, client count, or star rating]

SECTION 2: PROBLEM STATEMENT
  Name the reader's pain in their own words.
  Use PAS framework here.
  2-3 short paragraphs maximum.

SECTION 3: SOLUTION OVERVIEW
  What you do and how it solves the problem.
  Use FAB framework for each service/feature listed.
  Icons or cards, not walls of text.

SECTION 4: SOCIAL PROOF
  Client testimonials with real names and companies.
  Case study snippet with specific numbers.
  "From X to Y in Z months" format works best.

SECTION 5: HOW IT WORKS
  3-5 numbered steps.
  Keep each step to one sentence.
  Visual icons or illustrations alongside.

SECTION 6: FAQ
  4-6 questions that address buying objections.
  "How much does it cost?", "How long does it take?",
  "What if it doesn't work?" — answer directly.

SECTION 7: FINAL CTA
  Repeat the hero CTA.
  Add urgency if genuine (limited spots, seasonal offer).
  Include phone number and WhatsApp link for Indian audiences.
```

### Landing Page Copy Rules

1. **One page = one goal.** Do not split the reader's attention across multiple CTAs.
2. **Above the fold decides everything.** If the hero section does not communicate "what you do" and "why it matters" in 5 seconds, redesign it.
3. **Social proof is not optional.** No testimonials? No case studies? Then the page is not ready to launch.
4. **Mobile first.** 70%+ of Indian traffic is mobile. If your copy looks like a wall of text on a phone screen, rewrite it shorter.
5. **Numbers convince.** "47 keywords ranked" beats "improved SEO performance." Always be specific.

## SRS Copywriting Principles — Never Violate

1. **Every heading gets a Snapshot.** 40-60 words, citation-ready, no exceptions.
2. **Never sound like a machine.** Run the AI-Safe Writing Protocol before every piece goes live.
3. **Indian English always.** Optimisation, not optimization. ₹25 lakh, not ₹2.5 million.
4. **Frameworks are mandatory.** Every piece of persuasive writing must use a named framework. If you cannot name which framework you applied, rewrite.
5. **One CTA per piece.** Do not confuse the reader with multiple actions. Pick one.
6. **Numbers beat adjectives.** "47 keywords ranked in 90 days" beats "significantly improved SEO."
7. **Never keyword stuff.** If the keyword feels forced, rephrase the sentence until it does not.
8. **Content starts from activity.** Never invent content from nothing. It must trace back to a real audit, client insight, data point, or industry event (the ARC Engine).
9. **Write for the reader first.** SEO optimisation should be invisible. If a reader can feel the keywords, you have gone too far.
10. **Quality is non-negotiable.** A mediocre blog post damages the client's brand and SRS's reputation. Either write it properly or do not write it at all.
